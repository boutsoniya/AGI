What happens when AI learns from AI-generated data?

If tomorrow's models learn from yesterday's model outputs, what happens to the information they inherit?

synthetic datatrainingmodel collapse
THE IDEA

Synthetic data is data generated by a model rather than collected directly from the world or labelled by people. It can be useful when real examples are scarce, expensive or sensitive. But if new models repeatedly train on outputs from earlier models without enough reliable real-world data, errors and narrow patterns can accumulate and some of the original diversity can be lost.

WHY IT MATTERS

AI-generated data is becoming part of how models are trained and improved. The key question is not whether synthetic data is always good or bad; it is how it is generated, filtered, mixed with real data, and evaluated. Carefully checked synthetic examples can help, while careless recursive reuse can degrade a model's coverage.

TRY THIS

Imagine training a model to describe animal photos. First train on varied real photos. Then generate descriptions with that model and use those descriptions to create another training set. Repeat the process. Predict which details might become exaggerated, simplified or disappear. What real examples and quality checks would you keep in the loop?

WHERE IT BREAKS

Synthetic data can reproduce mistakes, biases and blind spots from the model that generated it. Filtering may remove unusual but valid examples, and repeated training can reduce diversity. On the other hand, high-quality synthetic data can be valuable when its limits are measured rather than assumed away.

TAKEAWAY

Synthetic data is a tool, not a free replacement for reality. Track its origin, quality, diversity and effect on independent evaluations.

BEFORE YOU READ

A team wants to train the next model mostly on outputs from its current model. What should it do first?

A. Assume more generated data must improve it

B. Check quality, diversity and performance against independent real data

Answer: B. More data can repeat the same mistakes and narrow the examples a model sees. Measure what synthetic data adds—and what it may erase.
