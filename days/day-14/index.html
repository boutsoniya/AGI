Can AI recognise when it doesn't know?

A trustworthy AI needs more than answers. It needs a way to signal when the evidence is too weak to support one.

uncertaintyreliability
THE IDEA

A model's confidence in a generated answer is not automatically a reliable measure of whether that answer is correct. Uncertainty estimation asks what the system can actually justify from its evidence, and selective prediction lets it answer some cases while abstaining or asking for more information on others.

WHY IT MATTERS

In high-stakes or ambiguous situations, a confident guess can be worse than no answer. A system that communicates uncertainty clearly can route difficult cases to a person, gather missing evidence, or give a bounded answer instead of inventing certainty.

TRY THIS

Prepare 10 questions: a few easy, several ambiguous, and two whose answers are not present in the supplied material. For each, ask the model for an answer, its confidence, and the evidence it used. Check the answers independently. See whether confidence tracks correctness, and whether abstaining on weak-evidence questions improves reliability.

WHERE IT BREAKS

Self-reported confidence can be poorly calibrated, and a fluent explanation can sound like evidence without actually supporting the claim. Abstention also has a cost: if the threshold is too strict, the system may refuse useful questions.

TAKEAWAY

Reliability means knowing when the evidence is not enough.

BEFORE YOU READ

A model has no evidence for a claim. What is the better response?

A. Give the most plausible guess

B. State the uncertainty or ask for evidence

Answer: B. Right. A reliable system should communicate the evidence gap and either ask for more information or give a clearly bounded answer.
