Can an AI explain why it made a decision?

An explanation is useful only if it reveals what actually drove the decision—not just a convincing story told afterwards.

explainabilityinterpretability
THE IDEA

AI explanations come in different forms. A model can describe the evidence it used, highlight influential features, provide examples, or expose parts of its internal computation. These are not interchangeable: a simple explanation can be understandable without faithfully reflecting the model's actual process.

WHY IT MATTERS

When a system affects people, money, health or access to services, users need more than a prediction. They need to challenge errors, spot unfair patterns and understand when the output should not be trusted. But a polished explanation can create false confidence if it is not checked against the model.

TRY THIS

Choose a small classification task. Ask a model to explain one prediction, then change one input feature at a time and record whether the prediction changes as the explanation suggests. Compare its stated reasons with a simple feature-ablation test. Look for mismatches instead of judging the explanation by how persuasive it sounds.

WHERE IT BREAKS

Feature importance is not automatically causal, and explanations can change with the method used. A plausible post-hoc story may not faithfully describe the computation that produced the output.

TAKEAWAY

A convincing explanation is not the same as a faithful one.

BEFORE YOU READ

An AI gives a clear, confident explanation. What should you check next?

A. Whether the explanation sounds reasonable

B. Whether it matches the model's behaviour

Answer: B. Exactly. Test whether the stated reasons track changes in the model's actual predictions, and remember that no single explanation method answers every question.
